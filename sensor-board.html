Georgia Tech Solar Racing · Fall 2026

Analog sensor board

Taking a bare PCB to a finished, tested board.

  • RoleAssembly and testing
  • MethodsSolder paste, reflow, hand soldering
  • ToolsKiCad, multimeter, hot plate

The task

Another team member designed this board. My job was to build it: take the bare PCB and get every component on it, including resistors, capacitors, transistors, diodes, switches, USB ports, and the STM microcontroller.

How it went

I started by applying solder paste, placing the parts, and running the board through the reflow oven. That got a lot of the parts connected, but when I checked the board with a multimeter, some connections weren't made and a few parts had the wrong values.

So I went through it with a soldering iron, reworking joints and swapping parts where needed. For each component I checked continuity with the multimeter against the KiCad layout, making sure every GND pin connected to the other GND pins, every 5V pin to the other 5V pins, and so on.

What I learned

I made a lot of mistakes on this board, and that's where most of the learning came from. I now know my way around solder paste, the reflow oven, the hot plate, and hand soldering, and how to debug a board methodically against its design files. On the next one I'm aiming to be faster and more precise.
